1 ZrHx Sieverts Desorption Kinetics

Thermodynamics

The equilibrium hydrogen dissociation pressure $P_{\text{H}_2}$ above delta-phase zirconium hydride ($\text{ZrH}_x$, where $1.6 \le x \le 2.0$) governs hydrogen loss across cladding micro-flaws as a direct exponential function of core temperature $T$:

$$\ln \left( \frac{P_{\text{H}_2}}{P_0} \right) = 2 \ln \left( \frac{x}{2 - x} \right) + \frac{\Delta H_{\text{des}}(x)}{R\, T} - \frac{\Delta S_{\text{des}}}{R}$$

When local temperatures exceed $1150\text{ K}$, hydrogen dissociates into atomic $\text{H}^0$, diffusing across the barrier coating into the supersonic propellant flow. This shifts the crystal matrix from FCC ($\delta$) to FCT ($\epsilon$), triggering a 4.2% macroscopic volumetric shrinkage.

$$L_{\text{eff}}(\omega) = L_0 \left[ 1 - \kappa^2 \cdot \text{Re} \left( \frac{J_1(k r_c)}{k r_c \, J_0(k r_c)} \right) \right], \quad k = \frac{1 + j}{\delta_s} = \sqrt{j \omega \mu_0 \sigma_m}$$

Blindspot 1: Sensor Physics

The "Thermal Inertia" Trap

Legacy designs relied strictly on Type C/D tungsten-rhenium thermocouples or optical pyrometers. In high-flow ($>40\text{ kg/s}$) sonic hydrogen propellant, local convective cooling conceals internal hydride degassing. Blindspot 2: Neutronics Illusion

Ex-Core Flux Averaging

Conventional reactors use ex-core fission chambers situated outside the pressure vessel. These instruments measure globally averaged neutron leakage flux.
